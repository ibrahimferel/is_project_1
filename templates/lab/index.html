Which algorithms are fit for real use?

AlgorithmStatusWhere it is used hereWhy
AES-256 in CBC mode + HMAC-SHA256ModernAll stored data and files AES is the current standard (FIPS-197). CBC with a random IV hides patterns; the HMAC detects tampering. AES-GCM would be the usual modern choice and does both in one step.
PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA256, 600,000 iterationsAcceptablePassword hashes and the password-derived key Salted and deliberately slow. Argon2id is preferred for new systems because it is also memory-hard.
SHA-256 / HMAC-SHA256ModernInside HMAC and PBKDF2No practical attacks known. On its own, too fast for password storage.
AES in ECB modeInsecure modeLab onlyEqual blocks encrypt to equal ciphertext, so structure leaks.
DESBrokenComparison copy56-bit key, brute-forced in 1998 by the EFF's Deep Crack. Withdrawn as a standard in 2005.
Triple DES (3DES)DeprecatedNot used64-bit block (Sweet32 attack) and slow; disallowed by NIST for encryption after 2023.
RC4BrokenComparison copyBiased keystream, broke WEP Wi-Fi, prohibited in TLS by RFC 7465.
Home-made AES (this project)EducationalEverywhere AES is used Correct output (tested against official vectors) but not constant-time and slow. Real systems use audited libraries.
